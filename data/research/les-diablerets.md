Les Diablerets sits at 1200 m on the north side of the Diablerets massif, about 120 km from Geneva
airport and under 40 km from Montreux. It hosts the Diablerets Alpine Centre, an official Swiss Ski
training facility set up after the 2020 Winter Youth Olympic Games.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/diablerets and en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Diablerets

## 2. ACCESS

**By train (ASD).** The ASD line (Aigle-Le Sepey-Les Diablerets) connects from Aigle. Published
journey times to Les Diablerets: Lausanne 1 h 05, Montreux 1 h, Geneva 2 h 20, Paris (Gare de Lyon)
5 h 20 via Lausanne on TGV Lyria. Full timetables at cff.ch.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z116

**By car.** Via the A9 motorway, Aigle exit, then the valley road. Driving times: Geneva 1 h 35,
Chamonix 1 h 30, Zermatt 2 h, Paris 6 h 15. A Swiss motorway vignette (CHF 40, annual) is required.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z116

**By air.** Nearest airports: Sion 60 km, Geneva 120 km, Zurich 250 km.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z116

## 3. WHERE TO SLEEP

The tourist office lists 8 hotels for the village; areas below are grouped by what each hotel itself
says about its position.

**A. Village centre / near the lifts.**
Hotel Les Lilas, a chalet from 1891 with rooms renovated in 2010, sits in the heart of the ski
station within walking distance of the centre, 700 m to 1.5 km from several lifts
(alpesvaudoises.ch listing; coordinates 46.3565/7.1079 per the hotel's own site). The Glacier Hotel,
family-run for 40 years, has a pool and sauna. Hotel Les Sources is near the centre and the ski lifts,
with glacier views from its restaurant and bar.

**B. Historic village core.**
L'Hotel du Pillon is described by the tourist office as the oldest hotel in Les Diablerets, with
mountain views and morning sun. Auberge de la Poste is a "typical Swiss chalet" with wood-panelled
rooms and a Swiss-specialities restaurant. Hotel Le Chamois sits close to both hiking trails and the
ski slopes.

**C. Outside the main village.**
Auberge de l'Ours, in Vers-l'Eglise (the hamlet with the 1396 chapel), holds 14 Gault&Millau points.
Auberge Restaurant du Lac Retaud sits at the lake itself (1685 m), near Col du Pillon, with rooms
above its own mountain-cuisine restaurant.

Source for this section throughout: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/G4046 (tourist office hotel listing)

## 4. SEASONS

**Winter.** Les Diablerets links into the wider Glacier 3000/Alpes Vaudoises ski area; Glacier 3000
itself guarantees skiing November to April, weather permitting, from 2971 m at Scex Rouge. The
village's own toboggan run (piste de luge) is 7.2 km long, dropping from 1719 m to 1167 m (568 m of
vertical), open daily 09:00-16:30 snow-dependent, with night sessions under lights every Thursday.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z41 and alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/V1331

**Summer.** Hiking, via ferrata, mountain biking and Lac Retaud (1685 m, swimming, paddling, fishing,
crystal-clear water, surrounded by conifer forest, panoramic views of Scex Rouge and Glacier 3000).
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P8781

## 5. WHAT TO DO

**Walks (2).**
1. **Meilleret sector and Col de la Croix, via the Diablerets Express.** Green alpine pastures reached
   from the cable car, with routes toward the Creux de Champs cirque and the Pierredar refuge.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z218
2. **Lac Retaud.** Described by the tourist office as ideal for a family walk, at 1685 m near Col du
   Pillon.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P8781
   (Multi-day option: the Tour du Massif des Diablerets, a 4-5 day itinerary around the massif.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z218)

**Sights / rides (2).**
1. **Glacier 3000 cable car and Peak Walk by Tissot.** From Col du Pillon, the cable car reaches
   Scex Rouge at 2971 m in 15 minutes. The summit gives views of 24 peaks above 4000 m, including the
   Eiger, Monch, Jungfrau, Matterhorn, Grand Combin and Mont Blanc. The Peak Walk by Tissot is a
   107 m suspension bridge linking two summits at 3000 m, open year-round, described by the operator
   as unique in the world for linking two peaks. The Alpine Coaster is billed as the highest summer
   luge track.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z41 and alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P5496
2. **Via ferrata routes.** Three graded routes: Via ferrata des Dames Anglaises (0.35 km, 1.5 h,
   +275 m/-3 m); Via ferrata de la cascade du Dar (0.4 km, 1.5 h, +185 m/-256 m); Via ferrata du Rocher
   Jaune (0.83 km, 2 h, +283 m/-321 m).
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/G4041

**Restaurants (2), both listed by the tourist office.**
1. **La Belle Descente.** Described as the local restaurant with terroir and seasonal cuisine.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/G4042
2. **Auberge de l'Ours, Vers-l'Eglise.** 14 Gault&Millau points.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/G4046 and alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/G4042
   (At altitude: Restaurant Botta at Glacier 3000, designed by architect Mario Botta, three levels in
   the cable-car arrival building, billed as the highest restaurant in canton Vaud, with both
   table-service and self-service sections. Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P889)

**Typical to the place.**
**Vers-l'Eglise's 1396 chapel and the Musee des Ormonts**, a local heritage museum for the Ormonts
valley.
Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/diablerets

## 8. FAQ CANDIDATES

1. **How do you reach Les Diablerets by train?**
   The ASD line from Aigle; published journey times are Lausanne 1h05, Montreux 1h, Geneva 2h20.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z116
2. **How long is the cable car ride to Glacier 3000?**
   15 minutes from Col du Pillon to Scex Rouge at 2971 m.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/Z41
3. **What is the Peak Walk by Tissot?**
   A 107 m suspension bridge at 3000 m linking two summits at Glacier 3000, open year-round, billed by
   the operator as the only bridge of its kind in the world.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P5496
4. **Is there a toboggan run in Les Diablerets?**
   Yes, 7.2 km long, dropping from 1719 m to 1167 m, open daily 09:00-16:30 (snow-dependent), with
   night sessions every Thursday.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/V1331
5. **Can you swim near Les Diablerets in summer?**
   Yes, at Lac Retaud, 1685 m near Col du Pillon: swimming, paddling and fishing, with an auberge on
   site.
   Source: alpesvaudoises.ch/fr/P8781
