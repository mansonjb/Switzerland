## 2. ACCESS

**By air.** Geneve Aeroport has its own railway station directly beneath the terminal, reached from
both the Departures and Arrivals levels by a 5-minute walk. Trains to Geneve-Cornavin take **7 minutes**,
with up to **7 trains per hour** at peak times. Early trains run from 6am for first flights, and late
trains serve night arrivals. A Unireso zone 10 ticket for this journey costs CHF 3; a CHF 10 discount
applies to any train ticket to or from the airport as part of its sustainable-mobility policy.
Source: gva.ch/en/passengers/access-and-transport/trains/train-access

**By train.** Geneve-Cornavin is the main station, on the Swiss network and with direct international
services (SNCF into France, and others). Tourist information points operate at Cornavin, Monday to
Saturday 09:15-17:45 (Thursdays from 10:00), Sundays 10:00-16:00.
Source: geneve.com/en/plan-stay/tourist-information

**Free transport for every overnight guest.** Anyone staying in approved accommodation in the canton
receives a free Geneva Transport Card: unlimited travel for the length of the stay on Unireso buses
and trams (TPG), 2nd-class CFF trains within the zone, and the Mouettes Genevoises shuttle boats. It
is sent by email up to 3 days before arrival for use from the airport onward, is personal and
non-transferable, and is funded by the Geneva Tourism & Congress Foundation and Unireso at no cost to
the accommodation provider.
Source: geneve.com/en/plan-stay/getting-around/free-geneva-transport-card

## 3. WHERE TO SLEEP (three sectors)

**A. Vieille Ville (Old Town, left bank).**
The medieval hilltop core around Saint-Pierre Cathedral. Hotel Les Armures, in a 17th-century building
at Rue du Puits-Saint-Pierre 1, sits next to the Maison Tavel museum and the cathedral (lesarmures.ch).
Points: closest sector to the cathedral, Reformation Wall and Parc des Bastions; no lake frontage.

**B. Paquis / station (right bank, between Cornavin and the lake).**
The dense right-bank grid between the station and the harbour. 9Hotel Paquis, Rue de Berne 31, gives
300 m to Cornavin station or the bus station and 500 m to the lake (9hotelpaquis.ch); ibis Geneve
Centre Gare, Rue Voltaire 10, is a 5-minute walk from the station, city centre and lake (all.accor.com).
Points: shortest walk to the station and the Geneva Transport Card pickup, dense choice of budget to
mid-range hotels.

**C. Quai du Mont-Blanc / lakefront (right bank, facing the Jet d'Eau).**
The grand hotel row along the harbour. Hotel Beau-Rivage, Quai du Mont-Blanc 13, built in 1865, faces
the Jet d'Eau across the water (beau-rivage.com). ibis Geneve Centre Nations, Rue du Grand-Pre 33-35,
sits toward the UN/international organisations side, a short walk from Cornavin, described by the
operator as close to the UN and the CICG convention centre (all.accor.com).
Points: the Beau-Rivage side has the harbour view; the Nations side sits closer to the international
organisations than to the lake itself.

## 4. SEASONS

Geneva is a year-round city destination rather than a resort; there is no lift pass or snow report.
The Jet d'Eau, the Parc des Bastions and the CGN lake cruises operate through the seasons, weather
permitting; the Jet d'Eau specifically can be shut down in wind or cold (see section 5). The Escalade
festival runs in December and the Fetes de Geneve (when scheduled) runs in summer.

## 5. WHAT TO DO

**Walks (2).**
1. **Vieille Ville and Parc des Bastions.** The old town circuit around Saint-Pierre Cathedral, the
   Reformation Wall (Mur des Reformateurs, in the park since 1917) and the fortified Bastions, Geneva's
   largest and oldest city-centre park, created in 1726 and redesigned as a landscape park after the
   1849 demolition of the fortifications. The park holds over 200 tree species (about 140 labelled),
   giant outdoor chessboards, a music kiosk and, in season, an ice rink. Open 24 hours.
   Source: geneve.ch/promenade-bastions
2. **Quai du Mont-Blanc to the Jet d'Eau.** The lakefront promenade from the Beau-Rivage hotel row
   along the harbour (Rade) to the Jet d'Eau on Quai Gustave-Ador.
   Source: geneve.ch/jet-eau-geneve

**Sights (2).**
1. **Jet d'Eau.** 140 m high, projecting half a cubic metre (500 litres) of water per second at
   200 km/h, using two pump-motor groups of nearly 1000 kW combined. Created in 1886 as a industrial
   pressure-release valve, moved to its current harbour site in 1891, illuminated at night since 1930.
   Operation is weather-dependent (wind and cold cause shutdowns); current status is published by SIG.
   Source: geneve.ch/jet-eau-geneve
2. **Towers of Saint-Pierre Cathedral.** 157 steps to a 360-degree panorama over the city and lake.
   Open 10:00-17:30 October to March, 09:00-18:30 April to September. Adult CHF 7, AVS/senior CHF 5,
   youth CHF 4; combined tower and archaeological-site ticket CHF 12.
   Source: geneve.com/en/attractions/detail/towers-of-stpeters-cathedral

**Restaurants (2), both listed by Geneve Tourisme's own lakeside selection.**
1. **Restaurant de la Plage des Eaux-Vives.** 900 m2 lakeside terrace with a view of the Jet d'Eau,
   indoor dining and beach refreshment options.
   Source: geneve.com/en/eat-drink/restaurants/restaurants-with-lakeview-in-geneva
2. **Bains des Paquis.** Right-bank lake baths with a beach, restaurant, sauna and hammam, an
   established Geneva institution.
   Source: geneve.com/en/eat-drink/restaurants/restaurants-with-lakeview-in-geneva

**Typical to the place.**
**Course de l'Escalade / Fete de l'Escalade.** Running race through the Old Town on **5-6 December
2026** (48th edition), with the wider Escalade festival weekend running **11-13 December 2026**,
commemorating the failed surprise attack on the city in 1602.
Source: geneve.ch/agenda/course-escalade and geneve.ch/faire-geneve/decouvrir-geneve-quartiers/traditions-genevoises/fete-escalade

## 7. SWISS TRAVEL PASS

**Covered for lake transport.** The Swiss Travel Pass is valid on all CGN cruises on Lake Geneva
(except special event cruises), per the operator's own FAQ: "Yes, the Swiss Travel Pass is valid on
all our cruises (except for events). It is designed for foreign visitors to Switzerland." This
includes routes to French lakeside towns such as Evian-les-Bains and Yvoire.
Source: cgn.ch/en/faq
Museum and cathedral-tower coverage under the Swiss Travel Pass was not confirmed on an official page
in this pass; see UNVERIFIED.

## 8. FAQ CANDIDATES

1. **How fast is the train from Geneva Airport to the city centre?**
   7 minutes to Geneve-Cornavin, with up to 7 trains an hour at peak times; the station sits directly
   beneath the terminal.
   Source: gva.ch/en/passengers/access-and-transport/trains/train-access
2. **Do hotel guests get free public transport in Geneva?**
   Yes. Every guest in approved accommodation receives a free Geneva Transport Card by email before
   arrival, covering TPG buses/trams, 2nd-class CFF trains in the zone and the Mouettes Genevoises
   boats for the whole stay, at no cost to the guest or the accommodation.
   Source: geneve.com/en/plan-stay/getting-around/free-geneva-transport-card
3. **Is the Jet d'Eau always running?**
   No, it is weather-dependent: wind and cold can shut it down. When running it reaches 140 m,
   projecting 500 litres of water per second at 200 km/h.
   Source: geneve.ch/jet-eau-geneve
4. **Are Lake Geneva boat cruises covered by the Swiss Travel Pass?**
   Yes, on all regular CGN cruises except special events, according to CGN's own FAQ.
   Source: cgn.ch/en/faq
5. **What is the Escalade?**
   A December festival and footrace through the Old Town commemorating the failed 1602 surprise
   attack on Geneva; the 2026 race runs 5-6 December, with the wider festival weekend 11-13 December.
   Source: geneve.ch/agenda/course-escalade
